# Prism — Universal Video Menu (additive spec v0.1)

*Adds a launcher layer over the video player that already exists. Nothing here replaces the current player, its Netflix support, or its playback path — the menu sits in front of them and hands off to them. Where this document and the existing implementation disagree on mechanics, the implementation stands and the document gets a Decision: line.*

## 1. What it is

A Prism-rendered surface that aggregates rows from the services a household is signed into, and launches playback in the service's own player (the existing facet path). Read-only over the user's signed-in pages; observe-only, same posture as the ad engine (§26). Everything local; nothing transmitted (§22).

Live TV: the menu shows a service's *live now* row and opens that service's own guide inside its facet. A merged multi-service guide is deferred (separate future spec).

## 2. Rows (v1)

1. **Continue Watching** — merged across services, sorted by recency (see §4).
2. **My List / Watchlist** — merged.
3. **Live Now** — for live-capable services: channel strip, favorites first; "Guide" opens the service's guide in-facet.
4. **Services** — the catalog posters; tap enters the service natively. Always present; guarantees nothing is unreachable.
5. **Search** — v1 is chips: "Search on Netflix / Hulu / …" opening each service's search prefilled. Cross-service parallel search is a later option (heavy: N renderers per query). *Decision (2026-09-19): taken - see §7.*

Recommendation rows (each service's home rows) are **off by default**; if enabled, labeled by source ("Netflix suggests…") — provenance always visible.

Cards carry: title, poster **as served by the service in the signed-in session** (never cached beyond the session's own cache, never redistributed), service glyph, progress, and the deep link. Tap → the existing player opens that title in that service (promote to hero / fullscreen per current behavior). Back → menu.

## 3. Adapter contract (additive to existing adapters)

Optional per service — a service with no menu contract still works exactly as today, it just contributes no rows.

```
menu: {
  continueWatching(): Item[]     // {title, posterUrl, progress?, deepLink, lastWatched?}
  myList(): Item[]
  searchUrl(q): string
  liveNow?(): Channel[]          // live-capable services
  tune?(channel)
  needsAttention(): boolean      // login redirect / signed-out state
}
```

`lastWatched` is **optional and must be real** (from page data only) — never synthesized from row position. Adapters expose rows by reading the service's existing pages in the signed-in facet (its own surface or a hidden one); they never call service APIs on the user's behalf.

*Decision (2026-09-19, Phase 2):* the implementation stands on names. `continueWatching()` and `myList()` are the existing `videoLibrary` script (`window.__prismVideoLibrary.cache = {continue, list, shelves}`); `needsAttention()` is the existing session probe (`session.signedIn / signedOut`, the login-redirect settle in core); `searchUrl(q)` is a plain address field, `videoSearchUrl` with `{q}`, or - for a service whose search takes no words from an address (Hulu) or whose search page is a deep link the app will not mount (Peacock) - the `videoSearch` script (`window.__prismVideoSearch(q)`: the app's own search control from a mounted page, the person's words entered for them; core takes the screen to the service first when needed); `liveNow()` is the `videoLive` script (`window.__prismVideoLive.cache = [{id, name, url, now, logo, favorite}]`) and `tune(channel)` is the `videoTune` script (`window.__prismVideoTune(channelId)`: the channel's guide item pressed in the service's own page, a human's press from a Live now card; core takes the screen to the service and its guide first when needed). Every one is optional: a service with none of them contributes no rows and plays exactly as before (fixture: video-menu.test.ts). `shelves` (each service's own rows) are the recommendation rows of §2 — off by default.

## 4. Recency (the merge rule)

Services rarely expose timestamps, so ordering comes from three sources, in priority:

1. **Prism watch log** — every play that happens through Prism is stamped locally (Media Session / player state observed; on-device only, §22). Exact.
2. **Inferred** — first-seen time when a title first appears in a service's row; a jump to position 1 between refreshes means "just watched somewhere." Recent vs. not, no date.
3. **Within-service rank** — for the truly unknown.

Cards show recency only when it's real ("3 days ago · Netflix"); unknown shows nothing. Never invent a date.

*Decision (2026-09-19, Phase 2, normative — the transparency rule):* the menu's ordering is a **pure function of the four §4 inputs** — the services' rows as they gave them, the local watch log, the first-seen record, and now — and of nothing else (`packages/core/src/menu-order.ts`; fixture `menu-order.test.ts`: same inputs, same order; a card with no log entry carries no time). No ranking or recommendation logic beyond this rule and the person's own lists; no Prism-invented ordering, ever. Ties inside a recency class keep the services' own order, and the services are taken in the order the household added them (the App list) — a fact of the household, never what happens to be on the screen. The watch log lives under `video:log:<dashboard>` on the device and reaches no network payload (fixture). Recommendation rows are off by default; when on, every one is labeled with its source service ("Netflix suggests · …").

*Decision (2026-09-20, normative - the transparency rule, restated with lenses):* the menu's ordering is a **pure function of the §4 recency inputs and the chosen lens's named source data**, and of nothing else. No Prism-invented ranking, ever: with no lens, the §4 order (and A to Z for My list, per its Decision); with a lens, `orderByLens` in `packages/core/src/lenses.ts` over the rows and that source's numbers alone (fixture `lenses.test.ts`: same inputs, same order; no lens by default). Recommendation rows stay off by default; when enabled, every row is labeled with its source service.

## 4a. Lenses and ratings

*DRAFT written by the build from the 2026-09-20 addendum ("ADDENDUM - transparent ordering + ratings"): the addendum referred to a §4a that had not reached the repository, so this section records what was built and is for review; a correction lands as a Decision line here.*

A **lens** is a named, open source of numbers a person may lay over the menu's rows. **No lens is applied by default**: the default view is the person's own history and lists (§4). The picker sits over the menu; the active lens renders, on itself and not in any settings page, its **three-layer disclosure** - *what is counted*, *who is counted*, *who decides* - plus its **source**, a **one-sentence formula**, and the **date of the data**. The string "trending" never names a lens (fixture): a lens is named for what it counts.

*Decision (2026-09-21, lenses as rows - "Continue Watching and My List do not retain their context when moving away from Your Own Order. Please, instead, make those rows of carousels to look through below continue watching and my list carousels ... Like most read on wikipedia, award winners on wikidata, newest releases on wikidata"):* the picker strip is gone. **Continue watching and My list always keep the person's own order** (§4). **Every lens is a row of its own beneath them** - the household's titles (both rows, each title once) in the lens's order, the titles the lens has no number for left out, the lens's number under each card - with its formula, source and data date on the row's head and the three-layer disclosure on hover. The open lenses are read for every title whenever the menu opens (the cache first; Wikimedia and Wikidata directly, §22); the TMDB rows appear only under the household's key. A row whose sources are still reading says so on its head and fills in place as they answer; its order settles on the next open. `lensChoose` remains in core (a lens may still be named by the remote) but changes no row. Core: `videoMenuRows` -> `lensRows` (`LensRow`); host: `MainWindow.VideoHub.cs`; fixture `lenses.test.ts`.

*Decision (2026-09-22, the Library tab's sort - "sort options for the watch and library screens ... release date (desc/asc), average rating (desc)", then "now a lot of the lensed rows look the same. Maybe we should only do that in the library"):* **the Watch tab's rows keep their own order always** - §4 for Continue watching and My list, each lens's own for its row (a global sort there made every lens row the same row under different heads, and a lens row not in its lens's order says nothing). **The Library tab has four sorts as chips under its summary - A to Z, Newest, Oldest, Top rated - and the chosen one orders every genre row inside itself.** The date is Wikidata's publication date, else TMDB's release / first-air date, else the year; the rating is TMDB's mean with under fifty votes after the rest; a title without the datum follows the ones with it, in the order it had. The rule is `hub-sort.ts` (pure, fixture `hub-sort.test.ts`); core orders and remembers the choice on the device; the host draws the chips and asks. **The search filters** by genre (TMDB's genres on every catalog card) and by service: the chips are every genre and service the unfiltered results offer, so a press never empties the other group; `videoLookupView(genre, app)`.

*Decision (2026-09-22, Browse by genre - "let people browse by genre, need to use only transparent algorithms though, that's the rule"):* a third tab, **Browse**: a chip per genre (TMDB's genre list, a chip covering TMDB's movie and series ids for it), then **Yours** (the household's titles TMDB files under the genre, A to Z) and three rows of TMDB's catalog **on the household's services** - **Top rated** (mean vote, at least 200 votes), **Newest** (release / first-air date on or before today, at least 10 votes), **Most voted** (vote count). Each row is one visible number or date from a named source; its formula, source, region (US), floor and data date are on its head and its three-layer disclosure on hover, as a lens row. 'On your services' is JustWatch's listing through TMDB matched to the provider ids the adapters claim, under the **offer filter** - **Included** by default (a subscription, free, free with ads, or owned) or **Anything** (rent and buy too) - printed in each row's formula; the card says which offer each service has. **Never** TMDB's popularity sort, trending, 'for you' or 'because you watched'; a title whose own listing names none of the household's services is dropped, never guessed. A card plays through the catalog press (the service's own search resolves the exact title). Needs the household's TMDB key; without one only Yours shows. Core: `browse.ts` (fixture `browse.test.ts`).

*Decision (2026-09-22, Most read reads the catalog - "Why does my Most Read on Wikipedia lens only show 6 items? Most, I have watched before ... It's meant to help me discover more titles"; "the lens should be looking at all movies & TV I have access to through my various services"):* under the household's TMDB key, **Most read on Wikipedia is a catalog row, not a re-sort of the two rows.** The pool is English Wikipedia's 1,000 most-read articles on each of the last seven days (Wikimedia's pageviews *top* list; a past day's list is kept for good): the articles whose names say film or series ("(2025 film)", "(TV series)"), and the untagged ones in a day's top 200 whose page summary describes one ("Stranger_Things"; one paced read each, a month in the store), plus the household's own titles. Each is matched to TMDB through its Wikidata item (TMDB's find by Wikidata id - a person, a book or a season finds no film and drops out), kept only where the household's services carry it under the Browse offer filter (JustWatch's listing through TMDB; owned counts), and ordered by the one number the lens always had: the article's reads over the last seven full days. Cards are Browse's (the services and their offers on each, a press plays through the service's own search). The row fills as it is read and says how many candidates it has weighed; its head carries the formula, sources, offer and data date. The featured feed's *Most read* was tried and refuses an anonymous client (429). Without a key the lens stays over the household's own titles. Core: `most-read.ts` (fixture `most-read.test.ts`), orchestrator `videoMostRead`; host: the lens row draws Browse's cards when `catalog` is set.

*Decision (2026-09-22, one lens row on Watch - "Help me understand the point of 'First released, on Wikidata' showing just 4 titles. TMDB Community Rating - this section seems to be mostly things I've seen"; option 2 of three):* **the Watch tab carries one lens row, Most read on Wikipedia** (the catalog on the household's services, above). The other lenses had only re-sorted the household's own two rows by one number - a few titles, all already known - and Browse already reads the catalog on the household's services by rating, date and votes, genre by genre. TMDB's rating stays on every card; the lenses stay defined in `lenses.ts` (`lensChoose`, the remote) and are not drawn as rows. Core `WATCH_LENS`.

*Decision (2026-09-22, the parents guide - "look at why a movie is a certain rating like PG13 as sexual content, as an example, may be a bigger concern than violence"; "a modal window you can read for any movie you look up"):* every title card's context menu (right-click, the remote's menu key) opens **Parents guide**, a panel over the wall: the US rating and its board (MPA; the TV Parental Guidelines for a series), then five categories - Sex & nudity, Violence, Language, Alcohol drugs & smoking, Frightening & intense - each listing every ratings board that gave a reason under it, **in the board's own words with the board on the line** (Kijkwijzer, FSK, ClassInd, BBFC...), a reason the mapping does not place under *Other reasons* in its words, then every country's rating, then the method on the panel itself. The source is the boards' ratings and reasons as TMDB records them (`release_dates` / `content_ratings`, under the household's key); the mapping is by the reason's words (`parents-guide.ts`, fixture `parents-guide.test.ts`); nothing is scored or weighed. A category no board names says "No board lists it" and the panel says that is not a finding. **IMDb's Parents Guide is not read, paraphrased or interpreted**: IMDb's conditions of use forbid extracting it, and §4a keeps IMDb's content off the wall. *Coverage, measured on the Most read row the same day: 39 of 40 titles carry a US rating, 6 of 40 a board's reasons* - the panel is mostly the rating until more boards' reasons reach TMDB.

*Decision (2026-09-22, later the same day - reversed):* **the parents guide is removed** ("It really does nothing compared to the IMDB parents guide"; "get rid of the parents guide"). The ratings boards' reasons reached TMDB for 6 titles in 40, so the panel was mostly a rating and "No board lists it". No open or licensable source offers IMDb-style scene notes with severity votes (IMDb's terms forbid extracting its guide; Common Sense Media and Kids-In-Mind are licensed or closed), and a Prism-written guide (household notes, or a shared service with moderation) was not taken up. The panel, the card-menu item, `parents-guide.ts` and `videoParentsGuide` are gone; the decision above stays as the record of what was tried.

*Decision (2026-09-22, catalog card art - "some movie posters fit their cards but some do not"):* a catalog card (Browse, Most read, the catalog search) shows TMDB's landscape **backdrop** (w780), which fills the 16:9 card; a title with only a portrait poster shows it whole on the card's ground, never cropped to its middle. A service's own art fills as before.

**v1 lenses**, in the order they were built (open sources first):

| Lens | What is counted | Source | Key |
|---|---|---|---|
| Most read on Wikipedia | Views of the title's English Wikipedia article, last seven full days | Wikimedia REST API, pageviews per article | none |
| Award winners on Wikidata | 'Award received' statements on the title's Wikidata item | Wikidata P166 | none |
| First released, on Wikidata | The earliest 'publication date' on the item | Wikidata P577 | none |
| TMDB community rating | TMDB members' votes, one to ten; the mean with the vote count; under fifty votes sorts after | TMDB API vote_average / vote_count | the person's own TMDB key |
| Most voted on TMDB | The number of TMDB member votes | TMDB API vote_count | the person's own TMDB key |

Trakt is a later, optional lens (not in v1 code). Titles are matched to their sources by name: the English Wikipedia article (the plain name, then the usual "(film)" / "(TV series)" disambiguations, accepted only when the article reads as a film or a show), its Wikidata item, and the TMDB id Wikidata records (else TMDB's own find-by-IMDb-id, else its search by title). A title no source knows keeps its place after the ones it does.

**Ratings.** With a TMDB key configured, every card may carry TMDB's community mean and vote count, **always attributed and never a bare number**: `★ 7.8 · TMDB · 14k votes` (fixture: source and count or nothing). A press on the rating (the card's right-click / menu key) opens the full disclosure - the three layers, the source, the date - and outbound *See on IMDb* / *See on Letterboxd* links, **navigation only** (nothing is fetched from them; the addresses come from Wikidata's and TMDB's ids). Without a key there are no ratings, no TMDB lenses and **no TMDB call at all** (fixture). TMDB's attribution ("This product uses the TMDB API but is not endorsed or certified by TMDB.") shows wherever TMDB numbers show.

**Never surfaced**, and not to be added later - IMDb's score, Rotten Tomatoes, Metacritic, and Wikidata's mirrored review-score property (P444): their numbers are either licensed for redistribution by nobody but their owners, or aggregate other people's licensed numbers, and none of them can say who was counted - which is the whole point of a lens. `lenses.ts` carries this list in its header comment, pointing here.

**Where the data lives.** Everything is fetched on the device - Wikimedia and Wikidata with the shell's static fetch (an unparameterized https address, nothing appended), TMDB with the person's own key through the shell's keyed fetch (`net.fetchKeyed`: the address and headers as core built them; the shell never logs the headers) - cached in the wall's own store under `lens:<part>:<dashboard>:<title>` (articles and Wikidata seven days, reads and TMDB a day), and routed through nobody (§22). The TMDB key is kept under `lens:tmdb:key:<dashboard>` on the device; clearing it clears every TMDB number with it. It enters through the menu's own box, or - for a household away from the wall - through a file `tmdb.key` in the wall's data folder that the host reads once and deletes (an empty file clears the key).

*Decision (2026-09-20, "I don't want people to have to obtain a key for TMDB, this is only for me as the developer"):* the key a household needs is the **product's** - the developer's own TMDB application key, shipped as a build asset (`targets/win-host/PrismHost/Assets/tmdb.key`, git-ignored, never in the repository) and taken at boot when no household key is set. A household enters nothing. Every wall still calls TMDB directly under that key; nothing is routed through Entangled (§22). A household may still enter its own key, which stands over the product's, or clear it. The open-source build without the asset simply has no ratings and no TMDB lenses - and makes no TMDB call. The addendum's "user-supplied key" reads as this from here on.

*Decision (2026-09-20, later the same day - "It wants me to pay 149 per month for a commercial application"):* **reversed.** A key shipped inside the product is, in TMDB's terms, commercial use and priced as such; Prism ships no subscriptions and pays none. The shipped design is the addendum's: **each household's own free TMDB key, for personal use, optional.** The product carries no key; a wall without one has no ratings and no TMDB lenses and makes no TMDB call; the three open lenses need nothing. The build-asset path stays in the code but is unused and undocumented for households (a developer wall may still carry a key for its own testing under the developer's own personal account). The menu's key entry says plainly whose key it is. The lens choice itself is not persisted: every boot starts with none.

**TMDB's terms** (https://www.themoviedb.org/api-terms-of-use) are flagged for the household's review before any TMDB-backed feature ships in a build: the attribution requirement is met in the UI; the terms' limits on caching, commercial use and rate are the person's to accept when they enter their key.

## 5. Starting services & order

Hulu, Netflix, Peacock, Paramount+, HBO Max, Prime Video, Tubi.

- **Netflix** (already in the player) and **Hulu** (verified in WebView2) — first menu adapters, built on the known-good base.
- **Tubi** — free and ad-supported: proves the menu and intermission together on one account; recommended as the first *new* service.
- **Peacock, Paramount+, Prime Video, HBO Max** — run the WebView2 DRM check across all four in one pass *before* writing their adapters, so nothing is built for a service that needs the Edge hand-off. Notes: Prime Video's continue-watching mixes purchases/Prime/channels (tag the source) and is the likeliest to redirect to account pages (needsAttention matters); HBO Max redesigns often (expect churn); Peacock has live channels (liveNow row, not a guide).

## 6. Boundaries

- Playback never leaves the service's player. The menu launches; it doesn't play.

  *Decision (2026-09-20, the stage - "I thought we were going to have 1 stage where we watched all videos ... much like we're feeding all music through to 1 player with visuals"; "we only go to the full app when we need to configure something new"):* the picture is always the service's own player (their stream, their licence), and everything around it is Prism's, as the music stage is. A play, tune or search-open a person asks for enters the player's OWN fullscreen once it begins (`videoEnterStage`: the adapter's `videoCmd` fullscreen - VP-2's verb, a human's ask; the keeper's own `presentation` controls stay the keeper's alone, as `presentation-wiring.test` guards; nothing when there is no videoCmd, never a hunt beyond the names the adapter knows); the presentation keeper (§26, `keepPresentation` on the Video player's screen slot) holds it across ad boundaries and endings; the wall draws its own transport over it (the stage bar: the face, back / play / forward, next episode, captions, full screen, Watch) on a tap or the Watch tab; Esc / Back and the Watch tab return to the one menu. The service's own site shows only where it must: sign-in, a profile gate, a page Prism cannot read. Fixture: `video-stage.test.ts`.
  *Decision (2026-09-22, the Episodes menu - "a menu for episode selection ... ALL episodes and ALL seasons ... text plenty big so it would be readable on a TV from a distance ... a star next to each currently active season and episode"):* the stage bar carries **Episodes** while a series plays. It opens a panel over the wall, sized for the room: every season as an accordion (the playing one open, amber, starred), every episode a large row (the playing one starred); an episode opened in place shows its still, its synopsis and **Play episode**, which plays that episode through the pick's own path (the curtain, the stage). The list is the service's own when its adapter can read it - `videoEpisodes` on a hidden surface at `videoEpisodesUrl` ({id} = the playing title's id), the service's own ids being the only ones Play accepts - else TMDB's under the household's key, shown with Play off and the reason. Netflix reads its show page season by season (each season's ten, then its own "expand section"; Grey's Anatomy: 22 seasons, 466 episodes, 17 s). Core `videoEpisodes` / `videoPlayEpisode`, `seasonsFrom` / `currentEpisode` (fixture `episodes.test.ts`); host `MainWindow.Episodes.cs`.
  *Decision (2026-09-23, multiview - "We should really have up to 3 PIPs, and a button to turn it on/off and one for swapping the video of focus to the next service instantly"; "a quick change on the watch page too. So people can set their programs quickly for all 4"; the person's choices: pushed down, sound follows the big window, off closes the small windows, stacked down the right):* **Multiview** runs up to four services at once in the Video player: the big window in the screen slot's place and up to three small ones (16:9, a quarter of its width) down its right edge. The windows are the scene's screen slot and its floating video facets; core keeps their order and places them after the solve, so **Swap** (the next window becomes the big one) and a press on a small window **trade places of live pages - nothing reloads**, and the sound goes with the big window (a small window's own play never takes it). With multiview on, a pick goes to the **target** window: the big one by default (what was there moves down and keeps playing; past four the oldest small window closes, never the screen slot), or a small one named on Watch's **Play into** row - there a card press plays into that window, Watch stays up and the target steps to the next, so all four are set in a row. **Off** closes the small windows; the big one stays (returned to the screen slot at the title it was playing if it was a small window's service). The stage bar carries Multiview and Swap; videoServices().screen is the big window, so the stage bar and every verb follow it. Not kept across a restart. Core `videoMultiview` (on / off / swap / focus / target / state), `mvPipRect`; fixture `multiview.test.ts`; host `MainWindow.Multiview.cs`. Not yet walked with four streams playing (a service's stream limit and the machine's decode load are unmeasured).

  *Decision (2026-09-23, the TV off and on again - "even with this multiview mode configured, I'd like a restart to result in the same mode, # of videos, and specific videos playing as when it closed. Like turning off a tv and on again"):* the wall keeps, as it changes, what each video tile has **up** - the title's own address and its name (an ad changes nothing; the page leaving the title for another address clears it) - and multiview's on/off and window order (`video:up:<doc>`, `video:multiview:<doc>`). On the boot multiview comes back as it was and each tile that had a title up loads straight at that title's address; the service's own player resumes it there (the adapter's play script is not run - Netflix's would load the address twice). The title counts as a pick on its way - its name on the feed, the stage asked, so Watch does not cover it - and the big window (or a lone title) takes the sound once its page is up. This supersedes 2026-09-21's "a video service comes back on its home page" for a title that was up at close; a tile left on the service's own pages still comes back home. Fixture multiview.test.ts.

  *Decision (2026-09-23, "Shift + Pause = Pause all videos in multiscreen as well. Shift + Play = Play All, or still use the individual play/pause buttons as needed"):* with multiview on, Shift with Play / Pause - on the stage bar, the Watch page's now-playing row, a small window's own play button, or Shift+Space - sends the verb to every window; without Shift it reaches only the window it belongs to. Playing all never moves the sound: the big window keeps it.

  *Decision (2026-09-23, "When I turn off multiview, it should just hide the 3 windows, but instead it stopped the episode I was watching in the main screen from playing and it refreshed"):* **Off hides** the small windows: they pause and leave the wall with their pages kept, and the big window is not touched - no reload, its sound and its stage bar stay. **On** brings them back to their places and plays the ones the hide paused. While hidden, a pick plays in the big window (what was there moves to a hidden window). The old teardown (the small windows closed, a floating big window moved into the screen slot at its title - a reload) remains as the core action "close", unwired in the UI. Supersedes "Off closes the small windows" above.

  *Decision (2026-09-23, multiview by hand - "I want to be able to click/hold/drag a video card, and pull it onto Big Window, Window 2, Window 3, Window 4 areas ... And let me drag something OUT to get it back out of Multiview config"; "If someone pulls something out of the big window, backfill a small window item into it, if nothing is playing, then I guess just nothing is playing. Maybe we should show a picture of a movie stage, purple curtains"; the sound: "sound should always be on the main window"):* a card **held** (350 ms) or pulled up/down off its row lifts; a bar of the four places drops in at the top of the Watch page; let go on a place and the title plays there as a press with that place targeted would (multiview comes on first if it was off; the target goes back to what it was; Watch stays up). A quick sideways pull is still the row's scroll. A filled place in the strip lifts the same way: onto another place the two **trade places** (`videoMultiview("place", "tile:index")`); anywhere else it **leaves multiview** (`"remove"`): a small window's service closes; the big one out, the next backfills it and takes the sound; the screen slot, which cannot leave the scene, is **parked** - paused, off the wall, its page kept - and comes back when its service is picked into multiview again. The last one out leaves the big window **empty**: nothing plays, and the host draws the empty stage (purple drapes drawn in XAML, a spot, "Nothing is playing"; no image asset). A place with nothing in it never makes a frame on the wall. A small window, and the parked screen slot, never take the sound (claimAudio refuses them, B-290).

About 16:9, the small windows sit over the big one's right edge (shrunk to fit the height). Much wider than 16:9 (a 32:9 or 21:9 monitor), the big window is full height and exactly 16:9 on the left - no black bars - and the small ones fill the space to its right in the grid that makes them largest (`mvLayout`).

*Decision (2026-09-23, the page-only rule lifted for video - "Lets not carry this rule over for the video side. *Decision (2026-09-19, cross-service search - "All the services get searched but the app returns the results straight to the screen"):* taken out of the non-goals. **Search everywhere** (Enter in the Search row, or its chip) asks every signed-in service at once, each on its own *hidden* surface (kind `hidden`: parked off-canvas at full size, never revealed, muted) in the App's own profile - the service's search address with the words when it has one, else its home where the adapter's `videoLookup` script enters the words into the service's own control (`window.__prismVideoLookup(token, q)`, answered through the music-result channel with the token core handed the page; `packages/core/src/orchestrator.ts` videoLookupStart).
