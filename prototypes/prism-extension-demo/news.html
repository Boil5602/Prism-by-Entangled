Old ferry returns to the bay after nine years tied to the quay

Volunteers rebuilt the Marigold plank by plank. On Saturday she carries passengers to Tern Island again, and half the town has asked for a ticket.

By Odalys Brennick, harbour reporter
Photographs by Tovin Aldersea

For nine years the Marigold sat at the end of Chandler's Quay with a tarpaulin over her wheelhouse and gulls on her rail. This week the tarpaulin came off. The little ferry, built in the town in 1958, passed her survey on Monday and made her first crossing under her own power on Tuesday morning.

"She went like she had never stopped," said Corwin Halloway, who chairs the Marigold Trust and steered her out past the breakwater. "I had forty people on the quay and not one of them said a word until we cleared the light."

Four winters in a borrowed shed

The trust bought the ferry for one pound in 2019. The work took four winters in a shed lent by the Pellam boatyard, and more than two hundred volunteers. Schoolchildren from Quillhaven Primary sanded the benches. A retired engineer, Ines Varkady, rebuilt the engine from drawings she found in the county archive.

We did not restore a boat. We gave the town back its way to the island.Ines Varkady, volunteer engineer

Tern Island has had no regular service since the last operator closed. Its eleven households have relied on a mail launch twice a week. The trust plans three crossings a day from Saturday, with a fourth on market days, and says fares will stay under the price of the bus to Pellam.

What happens on Saturday

The first public crossing leaves Chandler's Quay at ten. The town band will play her out, and the harbour master has asked small boats to keep clear of the channel until she is past the light. Tickets for the day went in under an hour; the trust says more will be released on Friday evening.

Mr Halloway said the trust still needs deckhands for the summer. "If you can coil a rope and smile at a stranger, come and see us," he said.
